AC Leak Water Damage Help in Roseville

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Empty the Drain PanA pan you can get to safely should be emptied with towels or a wet vac, which ends the overflow. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. The air conditioner repair is typically excluded in both cases. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Our Process

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Measure the Spread

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • Poorly connected drain piping, which lets water out before it reaches the outlet.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

The Platform and Closet Walls

In a closet, the unit usually rests on a wooden stand built above the return air box. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.
